## SLOT: RESEARCH_SUMMARY
My research interests center on making bioprinting more precise, more accessible, and more biologically faithful. I approach bioprinting as a control engineering problem — one where the biology sets the constraints and the engineering provides the levers. Alongside that, I'm drawn to the clinical challenge of corneal tissue reconstruction, where the structural complexity of the tissue makes bioprinting both extraordinarily difficult and extraordinarily meaningful.

## SLOT: RESEARCH_TOPIC_1_TITLE
Control Engineering for Bioprinting

## SLOT: RESEARCH_TOPIC_1_DESC
Extrusion-based bioprinting is only as good as its consistency. Small variations in flow rate, nozzle pressure, or temperature can compromise cell viability and structural fidelity across a print. My focus is on applying closed-loop feedback control — PID controllers, real-time sensor integration, and adaptive parameter tuning — to regulate extrusion dynamics and produce repeatable, cell-friendly output. The goal is to treat the bioprinter not as a static machine but as a dynamic system that can sense and correct itself.

## SLOT: RESEARCH_TOPIC_1_TAGS
Closed-loop control, PID, Extrusion dynamics, Cell viability, Sensor integration, Real-time feedback

## SLOT: RESEARCH_TOPIC_2_TITLE
Cornea Bioprinting

## SLOT: RESEARCH_TOPIC_2_DESC
The cornea's highly organized, anisotropic collagen fiber structure and requirement for optical transparency make it one of the most technically demanding targets in tissue engineering. I'm fascinated by the challenge of recreating stromal architecture with sufficient fidelity for light transmission — and by the clinical urgency behind it, given global corneal transplant shortages. Cornea bioprinting sits at the frontier of where material science, cell biology, and precision manufacturing converge.

## SLOT: RESEARCH_TOPIC_2_TAGS
Corneal stroma, Collagen alignment, Keratocytes, Transparency, Tissue engineering

## SLOT: PROJECT_1_TITLE
Low-Cost Low-Shear Extrusion System

## SLOT: PROJECT_1_STATUS
Upcoming — Summer 2026

## SLOT: PROJECT_1_DESC
Extrusion-based bioprinters subject cells to significant mechanical stress as bioink is forced through narrow nozzles — shear forces that compromise membrane integrity and reduce post-print viability. Commercial solutions that address this are expensive and inaccessible to most research labs. This project aims to design and build a low-cost extrusion system that minimizes shear stress through careful geometry and pressure control, making cell-friendly bioprinting accessible without a large equipment budget. Development begins Summer 2026.

## SLOT: PROJECT_1_TAGS
Bioprinting, Hardware design, Extrusion, Shear stress, Cell viability, Low-cost instrumentation
